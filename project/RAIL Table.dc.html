9Motion spec · one document

Every animation in asks 1–8, with its trigger, duration and curve. Three rules make it a system rather than a list. Five named curves, drawn here as tick ramps because that is how this app draws measurement — tick spacing is the easing. A closed duration ladder: eleven stops, each with a job, and a transition outside them is a bug rather than a choice — with two declared exemptions, 0 · TRACK for anything following a finger 1:1, and meters (the clock, the cooldown) which run on elapsed time. And one exit — 180ms OUT, opacity plus a 4px translate, for every object in the app, so nothing ever leaves in a way you have to learn. Only two overshoots exist in the whole product (1.06× on a win, 1.04× on sitting down); nothing else bounces, ever.

9aRAIL motion · spec 01

8Profile, loadout, shop · earned vs purchased

One binary carries the whole progression system, so it is drawn structurally rather than decoratively: earned items wear the measurement ticks; purchased items wear a maker’s mark. Ticks are the app’s own language of record — the head strip, the slide rule, the reaction lane — so a ticked item reads as something the table measured out of real play. A mark is packaging: applied to the object, never cut into it. Nothing is sold with ticks, nothing bought can earn them, and the distinction survives at 24px on a stranger’s rail (8d). Earned ticks are bone; amber stays reserved for crowd and clock.

8aProfile · the record

A profile in a poker app is normally a trophy case. This one is a record: what you did, in the order it happened, with the rail counted as play. Earned and purchased get separate shelves — not because purchased is shameful, but because a shared shelf would let the two classes borrow each other’s meaning.

4Showdown and win · motion keyframes

Six frames of one 1.4s sequence, read left to right. The thesis: the pot numeral is the only thing that travels. Chips do not fly across the table — the number itself collapses, moves, and is absorbed into the winner's stack, which counts up and overshoots once. That is the whole celebration. It is satisfying because the money you were watching is literally the object that moves, and it is invisible by the hundredth viewing because nothing blocks the next deal: the sequence never covers the board, never takes the screen, and the runout keeps its position. 3Throwable targeting · three approaches + cooldown

Three genuinely different mechanics for the same job: pick a throwable, pick a victim, in portrait, mid-hand, without covering the table or firing by accident. Each card is the lower 620px of the table. My recommendation is 3b — it is the only one that is one continuous gesture, so the commit point is physical (you let go) rather than a second tap that can be mis-hit, and it never puts a finger over the board. 3a is the safest to build; 3c is the most novel but demands a learned mapping.

Cooldown is treated the same way in all three: a 2.5s hairline that drains under the throw key, with the numeral counting down in mono. It never greys the tray, never shows a spinner, and never nags — you can open the tray during cooldown and pre-aim, you just cannot release.

Every seat gets a bust — a 56px avatar frame set outside the hairline, index chipped into its corner, hole cards tucked behind.
